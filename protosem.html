Week 2 — Algorithmic Logic, Innovation 101 & Care Coordinator App

August 2026

Week 2 focused on foundational computing concepts, algorithmic logic, programming paradigms, and an intensive Innovation 101: Applied Design Thinking session. We started with real-world step-by-step logic exercises (analyzing the algorithm for how to make a dosa) and watched the "Think Like a Coder" series. We then transitioned into programming in Scratch, Python, and MIT App Inventor.

Project Highlight: Care Coordinator App (MIT App Inventor)

The Problem: When an elderly patient is discharged from the hospital, managing recovery — tracking medications, remembering follow-up appointments, and watching for warning signs — usually falls on family members who are often not physically present, medically trained, or given a structured way to stay organized. Paperwork and verbal instructions lead to missed medications, missed appointments, delayed responses, and caregiver anxiety.

The Solution: Care Coordinator centralizes a patient's post-discharge care plan in an accessible mobile interface. It sends timely reminders for medications and appointments, and provides a direct communication channel to a care coordinator — offering family members a single, reliable point of reference during post-hospital recovery.

Innovation 101: Applied Design Thinking & FIH Framework

By the end of Week 2, participated in an Innovation 101 session on Applied Design Thinking. We introduced the Forge Innovation Handbook (FIH) to analyze product innovation hypotheses across 5 core pillars:

  • 1. Problem Definition: Identifying real-world underserved pain points.
  • 2. Constraints & Barriers: Mapping technical, economic, and behavioral challenges.
  • 3. Value & Benefits: Quantifying outcomes and user gains.
  • 4. Solution & Usability: Defining the Minimum Viable Product (MVP) interface.
  • 5. Price & Target Economics: Evaluating cost viability and market adoption.

Using the FIH tool, we collaborated with the Beta Team to evaluate product hypotheses. We also studied foundational design thinking case studies:

  • IDEO Shopping Cart Video: Analyzing rapid prototyping and empathetic design from David Kelley (Father of Design Thinking).
  • Founding Stories: Market gap analysis of Airbnb and RedBus MVPs.
  • Previous Cohort Prototype: Gamified rehabilitation glove for stroke patients — an affordable, accessible alternative to expensive traditional physiotherapy.

Topics & Competencies Learned

  • Algorithmic Structure: Breaking real-life processes into discrete logic steps (e.g. Dosa preparation algorithm).
  • Computational Thinking: Logic decomposition via the "Think Like a Coder" framework.
  • Data Structures & Control Flow: Arrays, Slicing, Conditionals, Loops, Functions, File handling, High/Low-level languages.
  • Applied Design Thinking (FIH): Formulating Product Innovation Hypotheses (Problem, Constraints, Value, Solution, Price).
  • MVP & Innovation Case Studies: IDEO Shopping Cart, Airbnb/RedBus founding stories, and gamified stroke rehab gloves.
  • Rapid Prototyping Tools: Visual block logic in Scratch, Python scripting, and MIT App Inventor.

Key Takeaways

  • Defining an MVP early with the Forge Innovation Handbook (FIH) prevents over-engineering and aligns solutions with real user constraints.
  • Studying IDEO, Airbnb, and RedBus proves that empathetic observation and rapid prototyping drive successful innovation.
