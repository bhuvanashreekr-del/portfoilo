ProtoSem 1.18

Weekly Learning Journal

A running log from Forge, the Coimbatore Innovation & Business Incubator at KCT Tech Park — mentor-led, hands-on work on applied technology and innovation challenges, documented week by week.

This page is a static, manually-updated journal — no backend, no database, no build step. Every week is copied from the reusable template at the bottom of this file's source code, filled in by hand, and saved. Newest entries are added at the top of the list below.

Week 1

July 2026

5S and Hands-On Electronics Work

Worked with the electronics team this week and applied 5S principles to organize the workspace. Segregated ICs and sorted other soldering-related equipment such as the soldering iron and soldering flux into proper, labeled storage. Later in the week, shifted focus to building a personal portfolio site and learned how to structure and deliver a proper tech talk.

Topics Learned

  • Applying 5S (Sort, Set in order, Shine, Standardize, Sustain) to a real workspace
  • Segregating and organizing ICs by type/value
  • Proper storage and handling of soldering tools (soldering iron, soldering flux)
  • Building a personal portfolio from scratch
  • Structuring and delivering an effective tech talk

Key Takeaways

  • 5S isn't just theory — organizing a real electronics bench made the value click immediately
  • Small things like labeling and sorting save real time for the whole team, not just one person
  • Building a portfolio shifts focus toward presenting work clearly, not just doing it
  • A good tech talk is about clarity and structure, not just knowing the material

Reflection

Week 1 was where things got hands-on — 5S made a lot more sense once it was applied to a real bench instead of read about in theory. Building a personal portfolio also reframed how to think about the work itself: it's not just about doing the task, but being able to explain and present it clearly, which ties directly into what was learned about giving a proper tech talk.

Week 0

July 2026

16 Personalities Assessment

Kicked off ProtoSem with an onboarding session at Forge, followed by the 16 Personalities assessment as a way to understand working style, communication preferences, and how I'm likely to operate inside a mentor-led, team-based environment. The rest of the week was spent going through the cohort structure, meeting mentors, and mapping out what the coming weeks of applied technology work would look like.

Topics Learned

  • How the ProtoSem cohort is structured week to week
  • Interpreting a personality assessment as a working-style input, not a label
  • Setting expectations with mentors for applied, hands-on work
  • Basics of documenting progress in a public-facing weekly journal

Key Takeaways

  • Working style awareness is useful mainly as a conversation starter with mentors and teammates
  • A short weekly write-up is easier to keep up with than a long one — brevity is the plan going forward
  • Clear expectations in week 0 save time in every week that follows

Resources Used

16Personalities.com Forge onboarding deck

Reflection

Week 0 was less about technical output and more about calibration — understanding how I work before being handed a real problem to solve. Going into Week 1, I want to carry forward the habit of writing things down as I go, rather than reconstructing what happened at the end of the week.
